We develop AI and computational methods that make the rest of our work faster
and more scalable: tools that connect proteomics data to protein structure and
function, methods that make large models tractable to simulate, and automation
that shortens the cycle between modeling and experiment.

Selected work:

- **ProteoMeter**, a pipeline that integrates multi-PTM and limited proteolysis
  data to reveal modification–structure coupling at the residue level
  ([NAR Genomics and Bioinformatics, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nargab/lqag073)).
- **ProCaliper**, for functional and structural analysis, visualization, and
  annotation of proteins
  ([Bioinformatics Advances, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioadv/vbaf275)).
- Aggregation methods for quantifying PTM and structural changes in bottom-up
  proteomics ([J. Proteome Research, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jproteome.5c00782)).
- A review of how artificial intelligence is transforming post-translational
  modification research
  ([Bioengineering, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/bioengineering12010026)).
- **Decomprolute**, a benchmarking platform for multi-omics–based tumor
  deconvolution ([Cell Reports Methods, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2024.100708)).
- Hypergraph models of biological networks to identify genes critical to
  pathogenic viral response
  ([BMC Bioinformatics, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-021-04197-2)).
- Scaling methods for accelerating kinetic Monte Carlo simulations of chemical
  reaction networks ([J. Chem. Phys., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5096774)).

**Applications:** tumor deconvolution in cancer, and host responses to viral
infection.
