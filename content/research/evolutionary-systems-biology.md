Which network structures can produce a given behavior, and how readily does
evolution find them? We study how evolution shapes cellular networks under
biophysical and biochemical constraints, and which design principles it
discovers along the way. Our main tool is
[BioJazz](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkv595), a framework for the
*in silico* evolution of cellular networks with unbounded complexity using
rule-based modeling, combined with mathematical analysis of the resulting
networks.

Selected work:

- BioJazz: *in silico* evolution of cellular networks with unbounded
  complexity using rule-based modeling
  ([Nucleic Acids Research, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkv595)).
- *In silico* evolution of signaling networks with bistable response dynamics
  ([Methods Mol. Biol., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4939-9102-0_15)).
- A core signaling motif that displays multistability through multi-state
  enzymes
  ([J. R. Soc. Interface, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2016.0524)).
- Enzyme sequestration as a tuning point for the response dynamics of
  signaling networks
  ([PLoS Comput. Biol., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004918)).

Understanding the principles evolution has found lets us use them to design
biological systems beyond what evolution has explored.
