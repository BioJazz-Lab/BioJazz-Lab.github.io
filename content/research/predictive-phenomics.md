A phenotype emerges from many molecular layers acting together. We integrate
quantitative multi-omics measurements, especially proteomics and
phosphoproteomics, with mechanistic models of signaling and metabolism to
**predict how cells respond** to genetic, environmental, and drug
perturbations, and to identify which measurements matter most for those
predictions.

Selected work:

- A phosphoproteomics data resource for systems-level modeling of kinase
  signaling networks
  ([bioRxiv, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.08.03.551714)).
- Targeted quantification of protein phosphorylation and its contributions
  to mathematical modeling of signaling pathways
  ([Molecules, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules28031143)).
- Prediction of optimal drug schedules for controlling autophagy
  ([Scientific Reports, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-38763-9)).
- A cell population–resolved multi-omics atlas of the developing lung
  ([Am. J. Respir. Cell Mol. Biol., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1165/rcmb.2024-0105OC)).

**Applications:** drug scheduling in cancer, and lung development and disease.
